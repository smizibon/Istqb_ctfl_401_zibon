The confusable pairs — why each distinction exists

Read the essence, picture the analogy, then say the WHY out loud — if you can explain WHY, no twisted exam scenario can untwist it.

QA vs QCK2

QA improves the process so defects are prevented; QC checks the product to detect the defects that still got through.

Think of it like… a restaurant kitchen: QA fixes the kitchen — training the cooks, tightening recipes — so no bad dish gets cooked in the first place; QC is the chef tasting the dish before it leaves the pass. And tasting can't un-burn the soup.

Why it works this way: you cannot inspect quality into a product after the fact — detection alone only finds what prevention failed to stop, so ISTQB pins prevention to the process (QA) and detection to the product (QC).

⚠️ An option that says "testing ensures quality" is the classic trap — testing (QC) gives information about quality, it never guarantees it.

রেস্তোরাঁর রান্নাঘর: QA রান্নাঘর ঠিক করে — cook-দের প্রশিক্ষণ, recipe নিয়ন্ত্রণ — যাতে খারাপ পদ প্রথমেই না বানে; QC হলো পরিবেশনের আগে chef-এর চেখে দেখা। আর চেখে দেখলে পোড়া স্যুপ আর আগের অবস্থায় ফেরে না।
Severity vs priorityK2

Severity is the engineer's measurement of damage; priority is the manager's decision about the fix queue.

Think of it like… a hospital: severity is how badly the patient is injured, priority is who gets seen first. A paper cut on the CEO — low severity, high priority — jumps the queue while a grave-but-stable case waits.

Why it works this way: technical impact and business urgency are measured on different scales by different people, so ISTQB keeps them separate — the engineer cannot decide the business order, and the manager cannot rewrite the technical impact.

⚠️ The exam's favourite twist: "homepage typo the night before launch" — answer low severity, HIGH priority, and never assume high severity ⇒ high priority.

হাসপাতালের স্ট্যান্ডি: severity মানে রোগীর চোট কত গভীর (engineer মাপেন), priority মানে কাকে আগে দেখা হবে (manager ঠিক করেন) — CEO-র হাতে কাগজের কাটা অল্প severity, কিন্তু queue-তে একেবারে আগে।
Verification vs validationK2

Verification asks "did we build it right (per spec)?"; validation asks "did we build the right thing (per real needs)?"

Think of it like… a perfectly engineered bridge to the wrong island: verification checks the bridge — every weld matches the blueprint; validation checks the island — is this even where people need to go?

Why it works this way: a flawless build of a wrong specification is still a useless product, so ISTQB separates conformance-to-spec (verification, e.g. reviews) from fitness-for-purpose (validation, e.g. acceptance testing).

⚠️ A scenario where the system meets every documented requirement but users can't use it = validation failure — don't pick "verification gap".

নিখুঁত ব্রিজ, কিন্তু ভুল দ্বীপে: verification দেখে ব্রিজটা blueprint-এর সঙ্গে মিলছে কি না; validation দেখে দ্বীপটাই বা আদৌ মানুষের দরকারের জায়গা কি না।
Confirmation vs regression testingK2

Confirmation re-runs the failed test to prove that defect is fixed; regression re-runs the broader suite to prove the change broke nothing else.

Think of it like… fixing a jammed door: confirmation testing checks the door is now locked properly; regression testing checks the fix didn't jam a window elsewhere in the house.

Why it works this way: fixes are new code, and new code can break old things — a defect fix is itself a change, so ISTQB demands both proofs: the original worry resolved AND the rest of the house untouched.

⚠️ Exam scenario: "we only flipped a config flag — do we really need regression testing?" Answer: yes — a change is a change, and regression applies wherever testing applies.

আটকে যাওয়া দরজা সারানোর পরে: confirmation testing দেখে দরজাটা এখন ঠিকঠাক লাগছে কি না; regression testing দেখে সেই সারানোর কাজ ঘরের অন্য কোনো জানালা আটকে দিয়েছে কি না।
Test condition vs test caseK2

A test condition is the thing to test ("the worry"); a test case is the exact recipe — inputs, preconditions, expected result — that proves or disproves it.

Think of it like… cooking: the test condition is the worry on a sticky note — "does the cake rise?" — while the test case is the precise recipe with exact measures and the photo of what the finished cake must look like.

Why it works this way: you can't design or measure coverage against a vague worry — ISTQB splits analysis ("what to test": conditions from the basis) from design ("how to test": cases with expected results) so both are traceable.

⚠️ Exam scenario: a listed "test" with inputs but no expected result — it is not yet a test case; the prediction is the contract.

রান্নাঘর থেকে: test condition হলো sticky note-এ লেখা দুশ্চিন্তা — "cake ফুলবে তো?" — আর test case হলো নির্দিষ্ট recipe: exact মাপ, precondition, আর তৈরি cake-এর প্রত্যাশিত ছবি।
Entry vs exit criteriaK2

Entry criteria gate when testing may begin; exit criteria define when testing may be declared complete — both measurable, both agreed in the plan.

Think of it like… airport boarding: the entry criterion is the gate only opening when the plane is ready — boarding early means crowding a lounge that's still unset; the exit criterion is the doors closing only when the checklist is done, not when the crew "feels like it's fine".

Why it works this way: starting before the environment/build is ready burns days logging "defects" that are just unset setup, and finishing on a feeling ships risk — so ISTQB forces both gates to be objective and measurable.

⚠️ Exam scenario: "the QA lead ends testing once the system feels stable" — wrong; a sign-off needs countable evidence (tests run %, coverage, open criticals).

বিমানবন্দরের গেট: entry criterion মানে প্লেন প্রস্তুত হলেই গেট খোলে — আগেভাগে ঢুকলে অসাবধান পরিবেশে "defect" লিখে দিন নষ্ট; exit criterion মানে checklist শেষ হলে দরজা বন্ধ — ক্রু-র "মনে হয় ঠিক আছে" বলে নয়।
Error → defect → failure — and its caveatsK2

A human error plants a defect in the work product, which may — or may not — surface as an observed failure at runtime.

Think of it like… a water pipe with leaks both ways: an error is the plumber's bad joint (defect), but water may never flow through that stretch (defect never executed — no failure), while a burst can also come from outside the pipe entirely (environment). And if the measuring stick (oracle) is wrong, a perfect pipe still "fails" inspection.

Why it works this way: testing observes failures and works backwards to defects, so ISTQB warns the chain is leaky in both directions — defects can hide forever, failures can arrive without any defect, and a bad oracle fakes a failure in a perfect system.

⚠️ "Environmental conditions cause errors" = wrong — they cause failures; the error is always a human action.

দুই দিকেই ফুটো পাইপ: error মানে নল-মিস্ত্রির খারাপ জোড়া (defect), কিন্তু ওই অংশে জলই না চললে কিছু বোঝা যায় না (defect execute-ই হলো না), আবার পাইপের বাইরে থেকেও বিস্ফোরণ আসতে পারে (environment)। আর মাপার কাঠি (oracle) ভুল হলে নিখুঁত সিস্টেমও "failure" দেখায়।
Statement vs branch coverageK3

Statement coverage walks every line; branch coverage additionally walks every outcome of every decision — all forks taken both ways.

Think of it like… a maze of forks: statement coverage just visits every corridor at least once; branch coverage forces you to walk both directions of every fork. But even walking every corridor, you can still always turn left — one single pass with x=15 never tests the right turn (the false branch).

Why it works this way: a decision is a fork, and walking BOTH forks of every fork necessarily covers every line — so 100% branch coverage implies 100% statement coverage, never the other way round: full statement coverage can leave entire false-branches unexecuted.

⚠️ Give a one-condition snippet (if x>10…) and ask the coverage % of one test with x=15: statement = 100%, branch = 50%.

গলিপথের মোড়: statement coverage মানে প্রতিটা রাস্তায় অন্তত একবার হাঁটা; branch coverage মানে প্রতিটা মোড়ের দুই দিকেই হাঁটা। কিন্তু সব রাস্তায় হাঁটলেও তুমি হয়তো সবসময় বাঁয়ে মোড় নিচ্ছ (শুধু x=15) — ডান দিকটা (false branch) কখনোই পরীক্ষিত হলো না।
0-switch vs all-transitions vs N-switch coverageK3

0-switch proves each individual transition works; all-transitions is that full set; N-switch proves every sequence of N+1 transitions — chains, not just single hops.

Think of it like… a house of doorways: 0-switch checks every single doorway opens; 1-switch checks every doorway-then-doorway sequence — kitchen→lounge→cellar. Bugs rarely live in a door; they live in the hand-off: the state a door leaves you in silently breaks the next door.

Why it works this way: each transition can pass alone yet leave the system in a state the next transition doesn't expect, so ISTQB layers coverage by chain length — single hops (0-switch), all single hops (all-transitions), then longer chains (N-switch) to catch interaction defects between consecutive transitions.

⚠️ When a scenario says "the bug only appears when two steps happen back-to-back", the answer is N-switch (1-switch) — not 0-switch.

দরজার ঘর: 0-switch দেখে প্রতিটা দরজা আলাদাভাবে খোলে কি না; 1-switch দেখে দরজার-পরে-দরজার sequence — kitchen→lounge→cellar। বাগ সাধারণত দরজায় থাকে না, থাকে হ্যান্ড-অফে — একটা দরজা তোমাকে এমন state-এ ফেলে যাচ্ছে যেখানে পরের দরজা ভেঙে যায়।
Product vs project riskK2

Product risk = the thing you built can hurt someone (quality, harm to users); project risk = the building of it can fall apart (schedule, budget, staffing, politics).

Think of it like… a construction site: product risk is the finished bridge collapsing under a truck — the user suffers; project risk is the site running out of money, the crew quitting, the permit never arriving — the schedule suffers. The one-question test: "if this happens, does the user bleed or does the plan bleed?"

Why it works this way: the two risks demand different mitigations — product risk is reduced by better test design (more rigor where harm lives), project risk by management action (buffers, stakeholders, planning) — so ISTQB keeps them on separate lists with separate owners.

⚠️ "Delivering late" or "budget overrun" is project risk even though testing notices it first — don't classify anything a tester feels as product risk.

নির্মাণস্থল: product risk মানে তৈরি হওয়া ব্রিজ ট্রাকের নিচে ভেঙে পড়ে — ক্ষতি সোখে user; project risk মানে টাকা ফুরিয়ে যাওয়া, কর্মীরা চলে যাওয়া — ক্ষতি সোখে schedule। এক-লাইনের প্রশ্ন: "এটা ঘটলে user কষ্ট পায়, নাকি schedule?"
Why decision-table collapsing is legitimateK3

When a condition has no effect on the outcome, you merge its rows — you're not skipping tests, you're merging indistinguishable worlds.

Think of it like… a living room where the light switch doesn't affect the TV: one test of the TV proves it for both switch positions, because switch-up-world and switch-down-world are identical as far as the TV is concerned. Two worlds that behave the same are really one world wearing two hats.

Why it works this way: a collapsed rule's merged cells are don't-care values, and a don't-care by definition cannot change the action — so one execution carries the proof of both original rules. The test count shrinks (N rules → fewer), but the coverage of distinct behaviours stays complete.

⚠️ Collapsing only works on non-unique (don't-care) conditions — merging rows where every condition matters silently deletes genuinely different tests.

লিভিং রুমে যদি light switch-এর TV-র ওপর কোনো প্রভাব না থাকে, তাহলে TV-র একটা test-ই দুই অবস্থানের জন্যই প্রমাণ — switch-up আর switch-down আসলে দুটি পৃথিবী নয়, অভিন্ন পৃথিবী। তুমি test skip করছ না, তুমি অভিন্ন পৃথিবীগুলো merge করছ।
Test pyramid — why fat bottom, thin topK2

Many cheap, fast unit tests at the bottom; a few slow, expensive E2E tests at the top — the shape is a cost-and-feedback argument, not fashion.

Think of it like… building a wall: a unit test checks one brick in a second; an E2E test rebuilds the whole wall just to check one brick. So you check bricks at the brick pile (cheap, everywhere) and save whole-wall inspections for the rare things only a wall can reveal — does the roof sit on it.

Why it works this way: the higher the level, the more of the system each test drags in — slower, flakier, and harder to localise when red — so the pyramid maximises feedback per minute: broad certainty cheaply below, a handful of end-to-end confidence checks above, integration in between.

⚠️ The pyramid is a default, not a law — a UI-heavy legacy app with untestable units may justify an inverted shape; say "risk-based adaptation", never "E2E is bad".

দেয়াল গাঁথা: unit test এক সেকেন্ডে একটা ইট পরীক্ষা করে; E2E test একটা ইট পরীক্ষা করতে পুরো দেয়াল আবার তোলে। তাই নিচে হাজারো সস্তা brick-check, ওপরে মাত্র কয়েকটা বিরল wall-check — টেস্ট pyramid-এর আকারটা খরচের হিসাব, ফ্যাশন নয়।
Walkthrough vs technical reviewK2

A walkthrough is the author's guided tour — education, their document; a technical review is a qualified panel's inspection — findings and a verdict.

Think of it like… houses: a walkthrough is the owner walking guests through their own house, explaining each room — the goal is learning; a technical review is a licensed inspector team with a neutral guide checking against building codes — the goal is a verdict.

Why it works this way: different goals need different forms — the author leads, attendance is open, and there's no required verdict in a walkthrough (peer learning, defect gathering); a technical review needs qualified peers, defined roles including a neutral moderator, and documented findings against criteria, because its output feeds decisions.

⚠️ "Author leads, no verdict" = walkthrough even if experts attend; "defined reviewer roles + documented findings" = technical review even if it feels friendly.
